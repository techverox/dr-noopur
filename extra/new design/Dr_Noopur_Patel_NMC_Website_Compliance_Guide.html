Dr. Noopur Patel Website
Website Content & Structure Review

A client-friendly briefing on which planned website elements should be retained, revised, removed or held for compliance review following the NMC Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication dated 6 October 2026.

Prepared for: Dr. Noopur Patel Prepared by: DIGIVIGEE Review date: October 2026 Interactive • Print-ready
Important clarification: The new guidance does not automatically mean the entire website must be rebuilt or that every doctor website is prohibited. The planned site can still provide factual, verifiable patient information. The main review is about promotional framing, patient solicitation, doctor-image promotion, testimonials, outcome claims, patient material, rankings and lead-generation mechanics. The final treatment of each item depends on the exact guideline text and applicable state/clinical-establishment law.

1. Executive summary

What this means for the proposed Dr. Noopur Patel website.

Retain, with verification

Core information pages

About/profile facts, recognised qualifications, registration details, clinic/hospital location, contact details, consultation availability, factual treatment/condition information and educational articles.

Revise / review

Conversion-focused design

Hero messaging, repeated “Book Appointment” buttons, WhatsApp enquiry journeys, service-page CTAs, local SEO copy and any wording that could turn factual information into patient solicitation.

Remove / do not add

High-risk promotional content

Patient testimonials/stories used for promotion, before-and-after outcomes, “best/no. 1/100% cure” claims, success-rate bragging, fake reviews, paid doctor rankings and inducements.

Specific approval required

Patient media and clinical cases

Patient photos, videos, identifiable clinical details and operation footage must not be treated as safe merely because a consent form exists. Educational/scientific exceptions are narrow and require careful review.

4. Expandable compliance FAQs

Open each item for the practical interpretation.

No. The guideline expressly contemplates factual, objective and verifiable institutional information. Reassess individual pages and features rather than deleting the whole website by default. The rules for an RMP’s personal promotion and a hospital’s factual directory are not identical.

Factual information about services and medical conditions may be appropriate, subject to the rules. Write educational descriptions—not claims of superiority, guaranteed results, exceptional success rates or pressure to undergo procedures. The doctor must verify medical accuracy.

Do not rely on consent alone. The new guidance states that consent does not by itself make a prohibited testimonial, endorsement, promotional patient story or before-and-after depiction permissible. Keep testimonials out of promotional use unless qualified legal review confirms a specific lawful exception.

Do not treat every contact tool as automatically banned. Review the wording, placement, repetition, purpose and data collected. A neutral way to find contact details or consultation availability is different from aggressive solicitation or a lead-generation arrangement tied to patient referrals. Get a compliance review before launch.

The guideline distinguishes a factual hospital directory listing a doctor’s name, recognised qualifications, speciality, registration details and availability from paid/promotional advertising. Use of an RMP image may be restricted where it constitutes self-advertisement, solicitation or promotional publicity. Do not assume a large hero portrait or repeated portrait is automatically acceptable.

Only after moderation. An embedded feed can import prohibited content onto the website. SEO pages should provide accurate information about conditions, services and locations—not fake rankings, “best/no.1” claims, exaggerated outcomes or fear-based messaging.

The guidelines state that using a third-party agency does not by itself absolve the RMP or institution from responsibility for content it authorises, commissions, sponsors, adopts or knowingly permits. This does not automatically mean the agency has the same professional disciplinary liability as a doctor. Responsibilities and other legal exposure depend on the facts and applicable law.
